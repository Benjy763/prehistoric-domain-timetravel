How the globe works

Travel through time and explore prehistoric life where it lived. Pick a period at the top, turn the globe, and open a point to see the work behind it.

Two globes: Artists and Apprentices

The Artists and Apprentices switch on the globe chooses what you see. Artists shows the work of Prehistoric Domain's artists. Apprentices shows the pieces made by Academy students at the end of a course, pinned at the course's place and period. The two never mix.

The latest first, 50 at a time

For each period, the globe shows the 50 latest pieces, so it never gets crowded. The arrows next to the count step to older ones, 50 by 50. Choose 100 to see more at once.

Filters

  • Content types: show or hide Immersive (cube icon), Videos (play icon) and Images (picture icon).
  • My creations, once you are signed in: show only your own pieces, on either globe.
  • On a computer, you can also search the contents.

Our Continents and Real Land

Our Continents shows today's continents moved back to where they were at the time, so you can recognize Africa or South America in their ancient places. Points are shown in this view (Merdith et al. 2021 model).

Real Land, on a computer, shows the land that was actually above the sea at the time, without points (Cao et al. 2017, from the Permian to the Neogene).

Where the points are

Each point is placed where fossils of its species were found, then moved back in time with the drift of the tectonic plates. Species come from each piece's tags and are looked up in the Paleobiology Database (PBDB); points too close to each other are spread apart so each one stays visible.

Approximate places. Positions rely on scientific data and peer-reviewed models, with small adjustments so every point shows correctly. They are not exact prehistoric locations.

Points and colors

  • Every piece has the same pin; your favorites (the heart in a piece's window) are in ochre. To see only one kind of content, use the filters on the left.
  • Close points gather in a numbered circle that opens as you zoom in.
  • The speaker button at the top right turns the ambient sound on or off.

Time periods

Each period shows the approximate middle of that geological era.

Data: Merdith et al. (2021), Cao et al. (2017), PBDB, GPlates Project
