Understanding the Globe Views

About the point locations

Each point on the globe represents a piece of prehistoric life content (video, image, or 3D experience). The locations are determined by:

  • Fossil occurrence data from the Paleobiology Database (PBDB), matched to the species mentioned in each content's tags
  • Plate tectonic reconstruction using the GPlates API (Merdith et al. 2021 model) to reposition modern fossil coordinates to their ancient locations
  • Land validation ensures terrestrial species are placed on land, not ocean, at each time period

Points are only visible in the "Our Continents" view.

Our Continents View

This view shows the outlines of today's continents repositioned to where they were located in the past. It helps you recognize familiar landmasses (Africa, South America, etc.) in their ancient positions.

Merdith et al. 2021 plate tectonic reconstruction, extending back to 1 billion years ago.

Real Land View

This view shows the actual emerged landmasses as they existed during each period — the real ancient coastlines based on geological evidence. Sea levels were different, so the shapes differ significantly from modern continents.

Cao et al. 2017 paleogeographic reconstruction — available from Permian (~280 Ma) to Neogene (~6 Ma).

Time Periods

Each period button corresponds to the midpoint of that geological era. For example, "Jurassic" shows approximately 160 million years ago (middle of the Jurassic, 201–145 Ma).

Data: Merdith et al. (2021), Cao et al. (2017), PBDB, GPlates Project
