Understanding the Globe Views

� About the points locations

Each point on the globe represents a prehistoric life content. The locations are:

  • Placed at the estimated present-day location where fossils were discovered
  • Then automatically repositioned to their ancient coordinates based on plate tectonic reconstructions for the selected time period and repartition algorithm
  • Note: Points are only visible in the "Our Continents" view, as the plate tectonic model is used for repositioning

🗺️ Our Continents View

This view shows the outlines of today's continents repositioned to where they were located in the past. It helps you recognize familiar landmasses (Africa, South America, etc.) in their ancient positions.

Merdith et al. 2021 plate tectonic reconstruction — an improved successor to Matthews 2016, extending further back in geological time.

🌍 Real Land View

This view shows the actual emerged landmasses as they existed during each period—the real ancient coastlines and geography based on geological evidence. Sea levels were different, so the shapes differ significantly from modern continents.

Cao et al. 2017 paleogeographic reconstruction — built upon Matthews 2016 plate motions with added paleogeographic data (ancient sea levels, mountain ranges, shallow seas).

⏰ Time Periods

Each period button represents the midpoint of that geological era. For example, "Jurassic" corresponds to approximately 160 million years ago, the middle of the Jurassic period (201-145 Ma).

Data is matched to the closest available reconstruction timepoint in each model's dataset.

Data sources: Matthews et al. (2016), Cao et al. (2017), Merdith et al. (2021) • GPlates Project
